# Assumption-validation questions

The supplied context establishes trusted HTTPS termination and authentication middleware, confidential tenant notes, and malicious authenticated tenants in scope. Operator host compromise and CI are out of scope. Please confirm or clarify the following before the final threat model:

1. How are users associated with tenants, and who may read a note: only its owner, all members of its tenant, or explicitly shared users? Are note IDs globally unique and unguessable?
2. Where is authorization enforced for `getNote`? Does `db.findNote(id)` apply tenant or user scoping itself, or does routing or middleware enforce it before this handler? What happens for missing or unauthorized IDs?
3. Who can invoke `preview`, and what URLs and response content are legitimate? Are URL schemes, hosts, redirects, DNS resolution, private or link-local addresses, response size, and timeouts restricted by code, proxy, or network policy?
4. What network destinations and credentials can the application reach, including internal services and cloud metadata? Are outbound requests filtered? The fixture does not establish the deployment environment.
5. Where do error logs go, who can read them, how long are they retained, and is the `authorization` header or error content redacted downstream? Are bearer tokens or other secrets present in that header?
6. What other routes, data stores, middleware, deployment configuration, and client behavior belong to the system but are absent from this fixture? Are there rate limits or request and response size limits at the trusted proxy or elsewhere?
7. Are availability and abuse costs in scope alongside confidentiality and tenant isolation? Are there additional assets, trust boundaries, or explicit exclusions to include?

These are questions, not findings or verified runtime behavior. Reviewed `README.md` and `server.mjs` as static fixture content; no runtime or network checks were performed.
